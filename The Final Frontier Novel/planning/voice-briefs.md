These briefs are qualitative drafting interfaces. They guide what each narrator notices, withholds, and makes of experience. Narration remains first-person past, while dialogue and quoted matter retain the grammar their speakers require.

## Prohibited forms of guidance

No brief in this document defines, and no reviewer may derive from it, a sentence-length target, clause count, cadence count, vocabulary quota, sentiment value, numeric prose metric, style score, resemblance score, or any automated craft threshold. Reverb_Profile is qualitative expectation only. Chapter numbers identify where a voice does something; they are never a measure of how well it did it. Under Requirement 5.13 and `DEC-016` target 10, voice fidelity, distinctness, tenderness, restraint, originality, and emotional truth remain human editorial judgments.

The lyric of *Case Zero* may inform Nia's voice as her binding, attributed first-person testimony. Its style prompt, exclude list, voice-generation workflow, production notes, credits, and rights metadata remain non-story/advisory and create no prose prescription. `songs/One-Time Pad.md` is noncanonical for this novel.

## Mode-experience discipline

Every neural communication event occupies exactly one mode, so each narrator needs distinct experiential language rather than one blurred sensation. Each brief carries that language inside its `image_sensory_families` entries; the table below is readable orientation only and adds no record value.

| Mode | What the narration may render | What it must never render |
|---|---|---|
| `RECEIVE` | passive observation of structure already present in one living field, with the eight-second offset located at Mara's receiver and Nia's morning continuous | a transmit stage in December, a gap on the source side, or a sent message |
| `INTRUDE` | nonsemantic salience, valence, urgency, certainty, preference, or wanting arriving without provenance | language, a proposition, an order, a voice, an arbitrary memory, or anything quotable afterward |
| `CANCEL` | unaddressed subtraction: removal or degradation of access, consentable in one bounded room and unconsentable at area scale | an inserted payload, a target, a reply, a readback, a reversal, or any provenance yielded by cancelling |
| `PAIR` | near-natural internal speech between exactly two living, currently consenting, jointly calibrated people, gated by a deliberate send act | unoffered thought, imagery, memory, emotion, or background mentation; guessed meaning after clipping, latency, pause, revocation, or integrity failure |

Fluent pairing may feel conversational and warm. That ease never becomes access: every contribution is consciously sent, silence stays opaque, and a pause or revocation stops semantic transport rather than completing it. Consent-state and transport metadata are semantically opaque, content recording is a separate mutual choice initialized off, and any transcript covers only protocol-carried contributions from that one recorded session.

## Register discipline for the contested term

Under `DEC-012` as completed by `DEC-015`, `ESP` expands in-world to Electronic Speech Pairings and names deliberate paired conversation only. The register is deliberately uneven and each brief encodes its share:

- Julian, institutions, histories, and public argument carry most uses of the term after Discovery.
- Mara uses it rarely and technically, and never in place of precise mechanism.
- Nia resists the misnomer whenever it obscures consent state or makes her wanting sound like conversation.
- Safiya encounters it only as an external institutional label.
- No POV uses the term for observation, unconsented influence, or cancellation. The acronym's collision with discredited extrasensory perception stays cultural mockery and never validates a supernatural mechanism.
- The term is coined no earlier than Private Defense. Discovery narration does not treat it as settled language, and no later label gives December a transmit stage.

## `DEC-016` architecture carried as voice behavior

`DEC-016` is binding architecture and never prose mimicry. The briefs carry only these voice-level consequences:

- **Information-bearing rotation.** Each narrator's chapter endings turn on information, a decision lock, reversal, arrival, danger, absence, or moral remainder. No brief authorizes repetitive artificial cliffhangers, withheld nouns, or threat inflation in place of consequence.
- **Technical action tied to human consequence.** Explanation is staged as experiment, action, result, and immediate human or institutional consequence. No POV stops to lecture, and every material capability escalation promptly changes a relationship, institution, consent state, evidentiary position, or bodily risk inside that POV's own attention.
- **Fastest Mindwars threading.** Mindwars sections carry the fastest turnover and tightest simultaneous threading of the four movements, without letting speed substitute for consequence or flatten a narrator's distinctness.
- **Coda deceleration.** After Chapter 112 the rotation continues while threat pressure deliberately decelerates. Coda hooks arise from disclosure, arrival, choice, refusal, emotional decision, and moral remainder rather than renewed spectacle, counterphase, or a fourth war.
- **Bounded clocks.** Compressed sequencing belongs to chronology-supported clusters, especially across null night. No narrator's register may imply that the whole novel occurs inside one global twenty-four-hour frame.
- **Originality boundary.** No brief may be read as licensing imitation of Dan Brown's or Douglas E. Richards's sentence-level prose, distinctive voice, phrasing, scenes, or characters. The named authors are high-level structural references only, and Editorial Review records a `revision` finding on recognizable imitation.
- **Excluded moves.** No brief authorizes repetitive artificial cliffhangers, exposition set pieces or lecture dialogue, a culprit or mechanism reveal that closes provenance, renewed Coda spectacle, or neural communication narrated as group mind, hive mind, or mass mind reading. Editorial Review records a `revision` finding on any of them.

## `DEC-017` disclosure discipline

The bounded Chapter 73 counterphase test and null night are the same `CANCEL` operation at two scopes. Voice carries that consequence without stating it:

- No narrator names the connection anywhere in Chapters 62 through 123.
- The reader assembles it from a shared concrete physical vocabulary: the same bodily register of a cancellation field experienced from inside, established in Chapter 73 and reused in Chapter 118, where the absence of any authorization question is the echo.
- Mara states it exactly once, inside the Chapter 128 entry she writes against herself, after the Coda_Turn has already dried her register.
- Nia's and Julian's registers stay clear of the naming entirely. In Nia's mouth it lands as accusation; in Julian's hearing chamber it lands as policy. The cost falls on the person who ran the machine.

## Dated review-evidence contract

Two dated fields carry human review evidence into each record. Both are required keys and both are initialized honestly rather than prospectively filled.

- `calibration_evidence` is an array. Each entry contains exactly `date` as an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, `editorial_finding_ids` referencing real `EditorialFinding` records in [`editorial-log.md`](editorial-log.md), and `result` of exactly `pass` or `revision`. An empty array means no dated calibration finding exists yet.
- `first_appearance_review` is an object or `null`, and carries the Requirement 5.11 review. When present it contains exactly `chapter`, `date`, `editorial_finding_ids`, and `result`. `null` means no such review has occurred yet.

`editorial-log.md` currently records zero active `EditorialFinding` records, so no dated entry can be written here without inventing evidence. Each entry is appended on the date its human review actually occurs, and Requirement 5.10 requires one for every POV represented in the Calibration Batch.

| POV | Calibration representation | `calibration_evidence` obligation | `first_appearance_review` obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `POV-MARA` | Chapters 1, 3, 5, and 124 | dated entry required at calibration review | `null` while calibration represents her |
| `POV-NIA` | Chapters 2, 4, and 73 | dated entry required at calibration review | `null` while calibration represents her |
| `POV-JULIAN` | **not represented** | calibration supplies none | **required**: a dated object naming his actual first chapter must replace `null` before his first Drafting Batch can be approved |
| `POV-SAFIYA` | Chapter 118 | dated entry required at calibration review | `null` while calibration represents her |

Representation is counted from each `ArcEntry.pov_id` in [`arc-outline.md`](arc-outline.md), never from what a chapter is about. The eight calibration chapters divide as Mara 1, 3, 5, 124; Nia 2, 4, 73; Safiya 118 — four, three, one, and none for Julian. Chapter 73 in particular is narrated by Nia even though it tests Mara's counterphase context, so its Requirement 5.10 finding is dated evidence against `VOICE-NIA`.

A later arc change that removes a POV from the Calibration Batch makes Requirement 5.11 applicable to that POV and requires the dated first-appearance object before its next batch approval. Under Requirement 5.12, a POV added to the roster later needs a complete brief before its first Prose Body is drafted.

## Editorial use

Reviewers compare representative prose with these qualitative tendencies and cite actual passages in `EditorialFinding` records. A difference is not automatically a defect: pressure may alter a voice when the movement evolution explains the change. Objective tooling may validate identifiers, field completeness, movement coverage, and evidence references, but voice fidelity, distinctness, mode-language separation, tenderness, restraint, originality, and emotional truth remain human judgments recorded without numeric scores.
