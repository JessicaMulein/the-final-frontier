The copper fingers along the edge of the door met the frame one row at a time, a soft ascending sequence that ended at the latch. Then the ventilation baffle above me wound down, because the first measurement needed the room on its own, and I could hear the fabric of my own sleeve.

I sat in the chair. The clock beside the lamp said 9:41. It was a wind-up clock with no radio in it, which somebody had chosen on purpose, and it was four minutes slow.

The walls were not solid copper. They were mesh held between pale panels, visible through narrow inspection strips at each seam, every crossing of wire making a small square. The floor carried the same mesh under a removable surface. At the corners, braided straps joined wall to wall and wall to ceiling. Nothing here depended on paint looking continuous. A lamp ran off a battery on the floor. The emergency release was a red handle on my side of the door.

Through the observation window, Mara held up one finger.

First interval. I listened for an arrival, which is not how the measurement worked. The room could not prove what had happened to me before, and the instruments outside could not ask my mind whether it was being left alone. The receiving probe would compare the known Northline test field inside the enclosure against the same field outside it. I was not the detector. I had been told that twice and written it down once, and my body had not accepted it.

For weeks every small certainty had needed an audit. Turn left: why. Call this unit: why. Make tea: why this cup. I could recover reasons for nearly all of them, and recovery had become a second shift that started when the county one ended.

So I waited for the extra wanting to appear.

It did not.

That absence established nothing about the old event. It identified no source, and it did not prove the room could stop every field condition, or that any had been present before the door closed. I knew that at the time, and I want it in the account that I knew it.

My shoulders came down anyway.

Mara raised a card against the glass. **OUTSIDE REFERENCE STABLE.** Then another. **INSIDE BELOW RESOLUTION.** She did not write *nothing can get in*. Both cards said what the probes had measured under this configuration, and she had written them out beforehand in block capitals, which meant she had decided in advance what she would and would not claim to me through a window.

I put both feet flat on the floor. The headset pressure I still feel after a long shift was not there. Nobody breathed against one ear. No unit waited for a route, and no one needed my answer before I understood its beginning.

The relief was immediate enough to make me angry.

I had not known how much of me was braced against another finished certainty. I had been calling that bracing caution, because fear sounds like a finding other people can use. In the measured quiet it left my jaw first, then my hands, then the space behind my eyes, and for one whole minute I did not check myself.

Then Mara started the fan. Air came through the baffled vent with a low dry sound. She switched on the filtered light circuit, then the intercom carrier with no speech on it. Each service raised the floor a little. None of them restored the reference pattern inside.

At the final interval she spoke through the acoustic tube, which carries air and voice and no electrical path.

"Do you want the door open?"

The words came out of a small cone beside the frame, ordinary pressure in ordinary air. I knew where they had begun.

"Not yet."

She nodded, and I stayed another measured minute, and the thing I have not been able to put anywhere since is that the room was the first place since the routing decision where I did not feel required to establish the authorship of an impulse before I was allowed to have it. Quiet did not give me certainty back. It gave me a period in which certainty did not have to present identification.

Then the meaning changed, and it changed by arithmetic rather than feeling. The room worked because it had a boundary my apartment does not have. The county floor does not have it. My car, the grocery aisle, the shower, the road beside the crossing, the bed where I spent three nights rebuilding the quarry decision — none of them have it. Every ordinary room I had trusted because it had walls had been open to something walls do not govern. The copper did not create that. It measured it, by contrast, in me.

I looked at the red handle. If I pulled it, the seam would open and I could leave. That mattered. It did not make the outside less permeable.

When I did open it, the contact fingers separated in reverse order and the fan changed pitch, and Northline came back one electrical leak at a time.

Mara asked what the quiet had felt like.

"Relief," I said, and made her wait. "And proof."

She wrote both words down. Then, while she was capping the pen, she told me there was a page in her notebook headed *exposed persons*, and that my name was on it.

I asked for the exact heading. She gave it to me: two words, no verb, no date.

"Who else?"

"Mine. Above yours. And Ravi's, under protest."

"Whose protest?"

"His."

I have taken enough statements to know what a list like that does when a stranger reads it in five years. It will not read as a set of people one researcher could not yet rule out. It will read as a finding, because it is in the handwriting of the person who built the instrument, and because *exposed* is a word that arrives already meaning something. So what is the correct thing to ask for? Off the page, which changes nothing that happened? Or the rest of the names, which are not mine to have?

"Does the page have a date on it?"

She said it did not.

"Put one on it. And write down that it proves nothing, in your hand, on that page, not somewhere else."

She did it there, against the window ledge, and turned the notebook so I could see the line. It was a worse sentence than she usually writes. I did not ask her to improve it.

Then I went out to the car and sat in it for a while with both doors shut, which is not the same thing, and I knew it was not the same thing, and I sat there anyway until the windows went opaque.
