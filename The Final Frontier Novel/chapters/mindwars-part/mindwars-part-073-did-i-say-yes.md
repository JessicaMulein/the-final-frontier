My conditions were four. I gave them standing up, in the room, before anybody switched on the field.

One. Mark the boundary in metres on the floor plan and on the floor.

Two. I am the only person inside it. Not the only consenting person. The only person.

Three. My answer covers one run, now, and expires when the run ends.

Four. I hold the stop. If I use it, the field ends. No delay, no appeal, no explanation owed.

Halloran transcribed them. He put the stop second. I corrected the order and he started a fresh sheet. Conditions are sequence. Change the sequence and you change what they do.

Then he asked whether I was cleared for the floor.

"Badge question," I said. "Ask the person question."

He waited.

"You have established that I am allowed through the door. Knock anyway. Ask whether I agree to what happens after it. Wait for the answer."

Someone behind him said the incoming pattern would not wait for a seminar.

Venn looked at the countdown and said, "We have time for a question."

Halloran lowered the clipboard. "Nia Calder, do you consent to one bounded counterphase run under the four conditions recorded here?"

Before I answered, someone pointed to the authorization sheet. Three signatures. My name typed in the participant box. Properly filed.

Did I say yes?

No one answered for me.

Authorization described what the institution had decided. My answer described what could be done to me. The distinction took one question and less time than the argument against asking it.

At eleven minutes past four I said yes aloud to Halloran. One run. Four-metre boundary. Stop in my left hand. The answer expired at the end of the run. He read the conditions back in order, wrote the time beside my answer, and asked me to confirm. I confirmed.

That was the consent. Not my badge, not the three signatures, not the channel.

Venn and I used the paired channel to coordinate the run. We had calibrated together over two days. The calibration belonged to us as a pair; neither of us could substitute another person. Each contribution still required a send. Ease did not turn it into access.

I sent: *boundary at four metres, confirm.*

She sent the confirmation.

I did not send what I thought about the authorization form. I did not send the calculation I was making about whether I would be fit for a console after exposure. Those stayed where they began. The channel carried only the pieces we each pushed through the gate.

I paused it in the middle of the boundary check. Transport stopped at once. Four seconds later I reopened it. Nothing queued behind the pause and nothing arrived late.

The metadata retained start, pause, resumption, consent state, timing, and volume. It did not retain what I withheld or what the sent contributions meant. Content recording was off; neither of us had agreed to it. There was no transcript for a committee to reinterpret later.

Then Halloran checked my spoken yes one last time. I held up the stop. Venn started the field.

The room went level.

Not quiet. Quiet has distance and direction. This had neither. Every small unevenness in my head came to one height, as if a straightedge had passed across the whole interior and left no edge above it.

Nothing hurt. There was no tearing, pressure, static, or warning sensation. The extractor fan kept turning over the bench. I heard each revolution, but it was the only textured thing left. For nineteen seconds the fan continued and everything behind my eyes lay flat beneath it.

Then the field ended. I did not use the stop.

Halloran asked for orientation. Name, place, time, condition. I answered all four. Venn asked whether anything had arrived. Nothing had. Asked whether I could identify anything missing, I could not.

Forty minutes later I found the cost in the corridor.

There is a sentence I use with callers when questions stop helping and one clear instruction has to carry the next minute. Six or seven words. Stress on the second. I know what it does to breathing on the far end of a line. I know when to use it and when not to.

I lifted the paper cup and went to say it under my breath.

The movement completed. My mouth reached the point where the sentence begins. No words followed.

It was not the tip of my tongue. A blocked word has pressure behind it. This had none. I could describe the sentence's purpose, rhythm, and consequence. I could not produce it.

I tried twice more. Same condition. Then I stopped testing and returned to the room.

The paired channel was still available. I left it closed. A missing phrase was not a contribution to the boundary check, and transport metadata was not going to acquire meaning merely because someone preferred a tidy file.

I reported the loss aloud.

Halloran wrote: *participant unable to access familiar operational phrase after exposure.* He read it back. I corrected *recall* to *access*. Recall would have claimed a cause we had not established.

Venn asked when I had last used it. I told her. She asked whether I wanted to attempt it again. I said no.

Nobody in that room could tell me whether the effect could be aimed, whether a wider field would behave the same way, or whether another procedure could alter what had happened. The run established only the local facts: one bounded volume, one consenting person, nineteen seconds, cancellation of the incoming pattern, and loss of access to one familiar phrase afterward. That was enough for the report. It was not a law about every future machine.

They asked whether to log the run as a success.

"Pattern cancelled?" I said.

"Yes."

"Boundary held?"

"Yes."

"Stop remained mine?"

"Yes."

"Then log the success. Add the phrase loss as a consequence. Do not put one in the notes and the other in the title."

Halloran did that.

Since the routing call, I had treated every important judgment as contaminated until somebody could prove where certainty came from. Proof had not arrived. It did not arrive in this room either.

What changed was smaller. I had set four conditions, heard them read back, answered for one act at one time, and retained the stop. Afterward, with one sentence missing, I could still examine the next decision by its conditions and consequences. Provenance remained unsettled. Judgment remained necessary.

Halloran asked whether I was fit to return to the console that evening.

I checked my name, place, time, and the phrase that would not come. I checked the fatigue in my hands and the fact that I had just lost an instruction I used under pressure.

"Not tonight," I said.

He wrote it down. I trusted the answer.
