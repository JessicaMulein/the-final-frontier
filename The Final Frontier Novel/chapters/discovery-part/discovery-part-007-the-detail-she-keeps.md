"Fourteen, confirm west service, approaching the maintenance gate."

"West service. Maintenance gate in sight."

On my screen her icon was on the east side of the reservoir, moving away from me at the speed of a vehicle that existed.

Condition: a welfare call at a locked pumping station, one caller waiting outside in the rain, no report of violence, weather closing in from the north. Consequence if I trusted the screen: I would send a second unit the long way round the water and leave Fourteen driving away from a gate she could already see.

Here is the rule, and it is not written anywhere. You do not argue with a driver about where she is. You can argue with her about what she should do next, and you can argue about the time, and you will lose both. But she has a windscreen and you have a picture of a windscreen.

"Ignore your display position," I said. "Continue to the maintenance gate. Mark on scene by voice."

She acknowledged without commenting on it, which I was grateful for, because a driver who wants to discuss the software mid-approach costs you forty seconds you will need later.

I switched the incident to manual location and entered *telemetry inconsistent with unit report*. Then I called the depot support desk and got the hold music, which is eleven seconds long and starts again in the middle of a phrase.

Dev leaned across from the next console while I waited. "Same frozen layer."

"It isn't frozen. It's moving."

"The road layer is frozen. The unit moves against the wrong road."

That was precise enough to be useful, so I said it to support in his words rather than mine, and made them open the old ticket instead of issuing a new one. People will always issue you a new one. A new ticket has no history and no embarrassment in it.

Ticket 4716. I wrote the number on a yellow strip and put it under the monitor, where nobody could close it by closing a window.

Fourteen reached the gate in three minutes. The caller had found the missing caretaker sitting against the pump-house wall, conscious and extremely embarrassed, holding one hand wrapped in a tea towel. A cut across the base of the thumb, from a padlock hasp, of the kind that bleeds impressively and needs almost nothing. The crew cleaned it, checked him over, established that he had been sitting down for twenty minutes because standing up had seemed like a lot, and drove him home.

No hospital. No second unit. No event anyone outside the room would have kept.

I kept the strip.

At ten past nine the road layer corrected itself. Unit Fourteen crossed the reservoir without moving. Her icon jumped from the east access road to the west gate and then resumed an honest route back toward town, as though it had been there all along and I had been the one having an episode. Dev made a small sound through his teeth.

"Screenshot," I said.

He took one. I added the time to 4716 and drew an arrow from *screen east* to *voice west*. The pen skipped on the word *voice*, leaving the o open, so I went over it once. A brown crescent from the bottom of my coffee cup touched the lower edge of the paper while I was writing.

Yellow paper. An open o corrected in blue. The coffee mark under it.

That is the part I kept, and it is not the part anybody would choose. Not the caretaker. Not the reservoir. Not even the screen being wrong, because systems are wrong constantly and a dispatcher who remembered every wrong system would have room for nothing else. The useful fact of the morning was smaller than any of that. A person in motion said where she was. I believed the person over the moving picture. That is all.

Then the shift continued, because it does.

A lift alarm with no answer at nine forty. A child locked in a bathroom at ten past ten, retrieved by his own mother before the crew arrived, which is the most common ending to that call and the one nobody expects. Two vehicles touching mirrors on the ring road at half eleven, both drivers ringing in as though impact altered priority.

At eleven thirty-seven, support closed 4716 as *unable to reproduce*.

I read that twice. It is a true sentence. They had not reproduced it. Neither had I, and I was not going to, because it would need the layer to freeze while a unit was crossing the reservoir while somebody was watching, and I could not arrange for those three things to coincide on request.

I reopened it and attached Dev's screenshot. At eleven forty-two they changed the status to *monitoring*, with a review date twenty-eight days out.

Sequence, correction, condition, consequence.

Nothing broke between those items. I did not lose a minute or feel one arrive late. I had no reason to examine the continuity of the shift, because continuity was the thing I was working with. The queue advanced. Unit statuses changed. People said where they were, and when the displays disagreed I asked again.

At lunch Dev pointed at the yellow strip with his fork.

"You know the ticket's in the system."

"The system closed it."

"It reopened."

"Because the paper told me the number."

He put his hands up and left it there, and then told me at some length about his sister's wedding car, which is not relevant except that it was the good half of the day.

I took the strip home by accident. It adhered to the cuff of my sleeve when I cleared the console, then fell beside the handbrake when I reached across for my bag. I found it under the car-park light: 4716, the repaired o, the arrow, the coffee crescent.

I could have thrown it away. The record was open, the screenshot attached, the caretaker at home with a plaster on his thumb. Keeping it proved nothing.

I slid it into the notebook with the earlier account of the crossing bell. The two pages have nothing in common except that each holds one detail more completely than the day required.

Twenty-eight days is a long time on a screen and no time at all on a rota. I was on that console for nineteen of them, and Fourteen had the reservoir run twice a week, and *monitoring* is not a word that describes anybody actually looking. So the question I could not answer, sitting in the car with the strip in my hand, was who would be at the desk the next time the picture and the person disagreed, and whether they would have a piece of paper.
