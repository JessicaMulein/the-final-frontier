The clock above the training-room door ran forty seconds fast. I had checked it against my phone while she was still speaking, because I wanted the time I wrote down to be one I could defend later. I laid the pen across the corner of the sheet and left it there.

"You know you transmitted," I said. "You know it used the address you built from me. You know when you did it."

"Yes."

"You don't know it reached me."

"No."

"You don't know something else did."

"No."

"Then those are two stories about my wanting. Neither of them is my answer. If it was you, there's a source and a mechanism and somebody to answer for it. If it was something else, I'm the opening item in a sequence other people get to explain. Neither account has any evidence inside my decision."

She tried to say that responsibility did not require proof.

"Your responsibility is yours," I said. "The injury is mine. Don't use one to take over the other."

The December observation was a violation I could describe cleanly. She had received the continuous field of my morning without my knowledge, the eight seconds belonged to her apparatus, and I had felt no gap, no arrival, no change in what I wanted. Learning about it made the morning exposed. It did not make the morning uncertain.

The later event was different, and the difference is the whole of it.

"When you received me, I still knew why I did what I did," I said. "When I routed that unit, I knew what I wanted and couldn't find the judgment that made me want it. You keep talking about a signal. I have to go back to a console and decide whether my own yes has a beginning I can trust."

Mara's hands stayed flat on her folders. She looked at them the way she looks at instruments, as if the correct arrangement might produce a third record.

I asked what Northline calls me in its notes. She said *source* in the December file and *possible affected subject* in the later one. The county notice says *dispatcher*. Her private notes have no stable label.

"They'll choose one," I said.

She did not deny it.

"Not case zero."

That was not a request for better terminology. A case begins after the person has been converted into evidence, and zero makes the conversion look inevitable, as though every later harm needs a first body to count from.

"You don't get to make me the proof that your machine works," I said. "You don't get to make me the proof that something else exists. And you don't get to make me the reason you confess."

She said my name. That was not absolution either, and I did not take the pen back off the sheet to soften it.

"Record what you did," I said. "Record what I reported. Stop before you write why."

She said she would.

"Then send me every page you file. All of it, not a summary. I want to read what I am in that file before anybody else reads it and decides."

She wrote the request down, which is how she agrees to things. She wrote it in the December folder, which is the one I had asked her to keep separate, and I watched her do it and said nothing, because I had run out of corrections I could make stick in one afternoon.

I have no way of knowing whether what arrives will be the whole file.
