[Intro — solo harp, completely dry]
(Spoken, close)
Eleven miles.
That is a measure.
It is not the story.

[Verse 1 — voice and harp, dry and close, no audible room beyond the singer]
There is a short way to say this. You already know the short way.
*I lost my mother's language on the night you fired the null.*
Every word is true. Together they are nowhere near enough.
A sentence can arrive intact and leave the life it came from standing outside.

I carry words for other people. I know what can cross unchanged.
I know when one mouth says *the room turned*
and another record gives back *she felt unwell.*
That is not the same answer. I stop them. We begin again.

No one stopped the sentence for me.

[Pre-Chorus — low cello enters, still no reverb]
Keep the line if you need it. Put my name beside it.
Only leave enough room around the words
for everything the words cannot contain.
I am not asking one sentence to carry all of it.
I am asking you to know it doesn't.

[Chorus — opens harmonically without growing in size]
Forgetting has friction.
I know the work of grief.
A name catches on something. A name comes back in the bath.
You pull; it hurts; it moves.
Forgetting has friction —
and this came away so clean
there was nothing left to pull.

[Verse 2 — harp alone, voice completely dry]
It was a Tuesday. The kettle was on.
Nothing came into the house. No voice. No sentence. No third presence.
Their account requires an arrival.
Mine does not.
The change went the other way.

My public language stayed. My work stayed.
I can still carry an answer across a table without reducing it.
Some private words still answered. Some did not.
The loss was partial.
Partial is not small.

At that hour I reached for the one
that held the difference between my name in any mouth
and my name in hers.
I knew its use. I knew the warmth it changed in me.
Knowing where a word belongs is not the same as having it.

[Hold — voice alone]
I began the familiar inward motion.

The motion finished.
The word did not arrive.

[Chorus]
Forgetting has friction.
I know the work of grief.
A name catches on something. A name comes back in the bath.
You pull; it hurts; it moves.
Forgetting has friction —
and this came away so clean

[Bridge — sparse piano and voice; no new room tone enters]
Do not make a virtue of the words I have not said.
Do not tell me wanting less would make the loss more true.
A counterfeit can wear the outline of mercy.
It would still be counterfeit.

What I would do if something answered
is mine to tell.
You do not get that answer here.

[Turn — voice and harp remain completely dry]
Listen. I can hear the document forming.
Eleven miles. Tuesday. Kettle. Mother. Null.
Every item accurate.
None of them is me.

Two people made a room no archive entered.
One voice is gone. One is speaking.
What is missing has no copy.
What remains has a speaker.

A record may hold my sentence.
It does not get to become my mouth.

[Final Chorus — a little more harmony, no choir, no swell, no vocal lift]
Forgetting has friction.
I know the work of grief.
A name catches on something. A name comes back in the bath.
You pull; it hurts; it moves.
Forgetting has friction —
and this came away so clean
there was nothing left to pull.

The sentence ends.
I don't.

[Outro — solo harp thinning, no static, no carrier tone, no kettle, no knock]
Some words stayed. I will not make a glossary.
The one for how she said my name did not.
I know the shape. I know the edge.
No one else gets to name it for me.

Eleven miles.
That is the measure.
I am not.
