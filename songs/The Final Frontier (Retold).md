Production notes

Style prompt:
harp-led cinematic chamber pop, mid-tempo, instructional but intimate, close dry female alto starting conversational and building to a desperate soaring final chorus, solo harp, grand piano, low cello drone, warm string orchestra, sparse frame drum, faint computer cooling fan noise as room tone, one mechanical keystroke to end, deep cathedral reverb on the choruses but bone-dry in the verses, awe mixed with dread, expansive yet claustrophobic, acoustic and organic meeting sterile electronic textures

Exclude styles:
fast tempo, frantic, relentless percussion, busy arrangement, dance beat, rock drums, distorted guitar, aggressive, sinister, triumphalist fanfare, happy, pop, choir, doubled vocals

Cycle note:
A standalone piece operating outside the primary narrative cycles, serving as the "primer" for the physics of this universe. It distills the core mechanics of the Mindwars for those who don't yet understand how software-defined radio works. It focuses purely on the realization that if math can become radio, and radio can become thought, then thought and bits are entirely interchangeable.

Carried motifs, deliberate and load-bearing:

- "The final frontier was never out there in the black" is borrowed directly from The Final Frontier's chorus to ground the song in established doctrine, but pivots the focus from the geopolitical shore to the biological one.  
  

- "Somebody built a radio out of arithmetic" from The Synaptic Frontier is expanded into the central thesis of the first verse to plainly explain software-defined radio (SDR).  
  

- The biological mechanics explicitly abandon the bone as a receiver. The poetic lore phrase "Spectrum to bone" is rewritten to "Spectrum to the soul," and the mechanics clarify that the skull is merely empty space the wave passes through. The active transaction belongs entirely to the neurons, the electrical current, and perception itself.

- "Keep the handle on your side" is placed at the very end as a desperate piece of advice, bridging the technical explanation back to Faraday's defensive isolation.  
  

- The room tone trades shortwave static and console squelches for a computer cooling fan and a final keystroke, emphasizing the "software" half of software-defined radio and the terrifyingly mundane tools required to breach a mind.
